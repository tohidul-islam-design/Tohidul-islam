The challenge

More than choosing a product.

A custom apparel order combines garment selection, artwork, printing methods, configuration, pricing, and review. Fundraising adds campaign creation, promotion, and supporter purchases.

Vivid Customs connects these tasks for individuals, businesses, schools, teams, and communities. My challenge was to make the different journeys feel like one coherent platform.

Design and implementation ownership

As Lead UI/UX Designer and Frontend Developer, I translated requirements into journeys, interfaces, and reusable Vue.js components. My scope covered UX strategy, information architecture, prototypes, design systems, responsive implementation, component states, and implementation QA.

Product architecture

Three audiences.
Connected journeys.

Apparel customer

Create something personal.

Discover → Browse → Select → Customize → Review → Order

Campaign organizer

Turn an idea into a fundraiser.

Create → Configure products → Set goals → Publish → Share → Track

Supporter

Connect a purchase to a purpose.

Discover campaign → Browse products → Select item → Purchase → Support

Service discovery connects printing and embroidery information with product selection, helping customers understand their options before starting.

Design decision / 01

Introduce one decision at a time.

I organized product discovery around scannable information: garment type, brand, colors, sizes, fabric, customization availability, and order requirements.

Once a product is selected, a progressive workflow introduces configuration decisions instead of exposing every option at once.

Translate printing knowledge into customer choices

Screen printing, direct-to-garment, direct-to-film, and embroidery have different use cases and compatibility. The experience helps customers understand these options without requiring printing expertise.

Customization sequence
  1. Choose the product
  2. Select a color
  3. Choose a printing method
  4. Add artwork
  5. Customize the product
  6. Review the result

The design goal: keep the garment, customization choices, pricing, and next action understandable throughout the journey.

Campaign setup
  1. Campaign information and goal
  2. Products, variations, and artwork
  3. Selling price and presentation
  4. Shipping options
  5. Review and publish

Progress and validation show what is complete and what needs attention before launch.

Design decision / 02

Make launching a campaign approachable.

I broke campaign creation into manageable steps so organizers can configure products, goals, artwork, pricing, presentation, and shipping without facing one complicated form.

After launch, the management experience brings campaign status, sales activity, fundraising progress, products, orders, supporter engagement, and earnings together.

Profit calculator: make estimates understandable

The profit calculator connects product cost, selling price, expected sales, and estimated profit. Organizers can explore the relationship between these inputs before committing to a campaign.

Design decision / 03

Connect the cause
with the cart.

Campaign storefronts bring purpose and products together. Supporters need to understand the campaign, who they are supporting, what they can buy, and how their purchase contributes.

Across regular orders and fundraising purchases, product details, variations, customization, quantity, pricing, cart, shipping, and checkout preserve context through to confirmation.

What am I ordering?
How is it customized?
What will it cost?
What happens next?

These questions guided the hierarchy of the commerce experience.

From Figma to Vue.js

The same owner.
From UX decisions to frontend components.

I implemented the approved designs as responsive Vue.js interfaces, aligning component architecture with the design system. Product cards, campaign cards, form controls, navigation, filters, tabs, modals, and progress indicators can carry different content and states without rebuilding each screen independently.

Design tokens → UI patterns

Typography, colors, spacing, grids, and reusable interaction patterns establish a consistent visual foundation.

UI patterns → Vue components

Component definitions account for content, responsive constraints, interaction states, and technical feasibility.

Vue components → product features

Shared components support catalog, customization, commerce, campaigns, and dashboard experiences.

Responsive behavior & quality

Design for the moments between screens.

I reorganized product grids, navigation, customization tools, forms, pricing, checkout, and campaign dashboards for available screen space and interaction method.

My scope included cross-browser testing, responsive implementation QA, accessibility considerations, and continuous UX refinement.

Reusable state patterns connect the design specification with the implemented behavior.

The outcome

A connected experience.
Designed and implemented.
