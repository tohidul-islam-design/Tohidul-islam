The challenge

A big life decision.
Too many disconnected steps.

Finding a program is only the beginning. Students also need to understand requirements, prepare documents, find funding, and manage deadlines.

StudyFound brings these tasks into an agent-free platform so students can manage their study-abroad journey independently.

My contribution

As Lead UI/UX Designer, I shaped product strategy, information architecture, end-to-end flows, responsive interfaces, reusable components, prototypes, and developer handoff.

The experience strategy

One journey.
A clear next step at every stage.

I connected discovery, preparation, and application management around student progress. Each stage answers: where am I, what do I need, and what comes next?

  1. 01Discover

    Search and filter programs.

  2. 02Evaluate

    Understand fit and requirements.

  3. 03Shortlist

    Save options to revisit.

  4. 04Prepare

    Gather and write documents.

  5. 05Apply

    Work through requirements.

  6. 06Track

    Follow progress and deadlines.

Design decision / 01

Make programs easier to find. And easier to understand.

I organized discovery around country, university, degree level, and field of study. Program details then follow a consistent reading order.

Why this structure?

Students need enough information to assess a program without facing every requirement at once. Progressive disclosure keeps exploration manageable and gives detailed information a predictable home.

Design decision / 02

Turn a shortlist into a plan.

Shortlisting lets students save interesting programs without committing immediately. The application manager connects those choices with requirements, documents, deadlines, and progress.

Why connect these tasks?

The design goal was to replace scattered bookmarks, notes, and spreadsheets with a structured workspace that makes the next action visible.

Design decision / 03

Guide the writing.
Keep the student in control.

In the Resume Builder, I separated content creation from presentation: guided inputs come before AI enhancement, customization, preview, and export.

For the SOP Builder, structured questions about background, motivation, experience, and goals give students a starting point for generating, editing, and reviewing their story.

“AI should assist the student’s story—not replace it.”
  1. Enter information
  2. AI enhancement
  3. Customize
  4. Preview
  5. Export
Design system & delivery

Consistency beyond a single screen.

I built reusable patterns across discovery, applications, and AI tools, then worked with product and engineering to translate the designs into implementation.

Reusable foundations

Typography, color, spacing, buttons, forms, cards, navigation, filters, and modals.

Complete interactions

Progress, statuses, contextual guidance, and empty, loading, success, and error states.

Responsive delivery

Reprioritized content for smaller screens, documented behavior, reviewed implementation, and refined designs around technical constraints.

The outcome

A connected self-application experience.

Discovery, shortlisting, preparation, AI documents, scholarships, and application management come together in one product experience.

My contribution was the structure and visual system connecting these workflows. This case study describes the design work and intended experience; measured usability or business outcomes are not reported.
